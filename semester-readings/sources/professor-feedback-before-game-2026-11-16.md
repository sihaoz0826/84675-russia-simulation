# Professor's feedback on our strategy, before the game

Email from Professor Justin Key Canfil to Team 2 (Russia) about our strategy brief, received before the first simulation session on Monday, November 16, 2026. The exact send time was not recorded. Shared in two parts; both are copied word for word below.

## Part 1

> But one concern I have with the approach taken here is that it risks missing the forest for the trees. For example---and this is just one illustration---the discussion of education policy focuses on precise die-roll calculations to recommend divesting from education because of the threat of brain drain. But this overlooks another potentially viable strategy: Russia could do precisely the opposite, opening its borders and investing heavily in tertiary education. Conceivably, it could even reverse the brain drain by attracting talent from elsewhere, particularly if other major-draw countries begin closing their doors due to fears of espionage.

## Part 2

> There are many ways to play the game effectively, and I'm not suggesting you need to change your strategy. I just want to encourage you to think beyond the numerical mechanics of the simulation and draw on the broader concepts, theories, and historical examples we've discussed in class. The point of the exercise isn't simply to find the mathematically optimal combination of moves (which is hard because it also depends on other teams' choices), but to develop your own strategic judgment about how your country should navigate the opportunities and constraints it faces.

## How we read it

1. **Big picture over dice math.** Our brief optimized against Russia's starting numbers and treated them as fixed. Talent flow actually depends on wealth and university strength relative to other countries, and on their border choices, so it can change during the game.
2. **A concrete alternative.** Instead of giving up on education, Russia could invest in universities and open its borders to reverse its brain drain, especially if the US closes over espionage fears. This matches course readings: Zweig on China's strategies to reverse brain drain, and Reif, "America's Coming Brain Drain."
3. **Judgment, not optimization.** He was not asking us to change the plan. He wanted our decisions explained with course concepts and adapted to what other teams do.
