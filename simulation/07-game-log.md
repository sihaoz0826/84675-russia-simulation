# Russia game log

A round-by-round record of what we entered, what happened, and why we decided what we did. Kept for the simulation report (due Friday, December 4), which asks how gameplay unfolded, what we believed about our position, and how and why our strategy changed.

The private and public logs on the game portal are the real results. The "Your Dice Rolls" table is only a preview, and twice it showed different rolls from the logs; this record uses the logs.

## Before the game: how the plan changed

| When | What we learned | What we changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nov 12-14 (brief) | Rulebook: saving converts 2/3 of unspent dice into wealth; Russia's talent drains 40% a round; closed countries can't be robbed | Brief strategy "save, then strike": save about three-quarters early, no education, free-ride on rivals' basic research, stay closed, spy on open countries |
| Nov 14 | Simulation runs Monday and Wednesday (Nov 11 announcement), so about 6-9 rounds | Kept saving through Round 3 |
| Nov 16, professor's comments | He warned against "missing the forest for the trees": Russia could do the opposite, opening its borders and investing in universities to reverse its brain drain, especially if big talent magnets close over espionage fears. He asked us to use course concepts and historical examples, not just dice math | Added a second path: buy tertiary education each round to build universities, and be ready to open if talent would flow in |
| Nov 16, in class | Only 2 rounds on Monday, about 3-4 on Wednesday, so about 5-6 rounds total | Re-ran the simulation: save only in Rounds 1-2, then spend from Round 3. Added 2 basic research in Round 1 so advanced tech opens sooner in a short game |

**Course concepts behind the plan:** free-riding on public basic research (the public-goods problem); tacit knowledge can't be stolen (Gilli & Gilli); reversing brain drain (Zweig on China's talent strategies; Reif, "America's Coming Brain Drain"); research security versus openness (NSPM-33, Walker-Munro).

## Round 1 (Monday, Nov 16)

**Starting position:** wealth 4, 8 dice, talent 5.0, know-how (TK) 7, closed, no points.

**What we entered**

| Item | Choice | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tertiary education | 1 | 1 |
| Basic research | 2 | 2 |
| Applied research | 2 low-risk (L) | 2 |
| Borders | Closed | 0 |
| Spies | None | 0 |
| Saved | 3 | |

**Reason submitted:** "We are saving to grow our economy and investing in universities now so Russia can reverse its brain drain and attract talent later. We buy two cheap research dice to build know-how and start a little basic research so advanced tech can open sooner, and there are no rival points to steal yet."

**Why:** the two cheapest research dice give guaranteed know-how plus points; no spies because nobody had points yet; one university purchase to test the professor's idea; a little basic research because the game turned out shorter than planned.

**Results**

| | Before | After | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Points (L/M/H) | 0/0/0 | 4/0/0 | Both L dice succeeded, 2 points each |
| Know-how | 7 | 10 | +2 from research dice, +1 from one basic research success |
| Own basic research | 0 | 1 | Rolls 6, 3 |
| Talent | 5.0 | 3.6 | (5 + 1) x 0.6 |
| University level (TE) | 2 | 2 | **Did not rise** after buying tertiary education |
| Next round | W 4 | W 6, 12 dice | 3 saved dice became +2 wealth |

**Rivals after Round 1:** China **opened** its borders and France **closed**. US talent jumped to 16.1. Basic research was low: US 1, France 1, China 0, so our effective basic research was only 2.5 (medium research needs 8).

**What we learned:** buying tertiary education did not raise our university level, so the "build universities" path looked weaker; we stopped buying education. France could no longer be robbed, so our spy targets became the US and China. Rivals were not paying for basic research, so we could not count on free-riding yet.

## Round 2 (Monday, Nov 16)

**What we entered**

| Item | Choice | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education | None | 0 |
| Basic research | 2 | 2 |
| Applied research | 2 L | 2 |
| Borders | **Opened** | 0 |
| Spies | US at L, China at L | 2 |
| Saved | 6 | |

**Reason submitted:** "We open our borders while we have little to steal, to test whether Russia can reverse its brain drain as our economy grows. We save one more round to grow our budget, build some basic research toward advanced tech, and spy at the low level on the open US and China, since closed France can't be robbed."

**Why we opened:** we had only 4 points, a steal only copies points (we lose nothing), and closing again is free and works the same round. Opening changes talent flow only from the next round, so this was a cheap way to test whether Russia's growing wealth could turn brain drain into talent inflow.

**Results**

| | Before | After | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Points (L/M/H) | 4/0/0 | 6/0/0 | 1 of 2 L dice succeeded |
| Know-how | 10 | 13 | +2 from research dice, +1 from one basic research success (rolls 1, 4) |
| Own basic research | 1 | 2 | |
| Talent | 3.6 | 2.0 | Still draining; the border change only affects talent flow from Round 3 |
| Borders | Closed | Open | |
| Spy on US (L) | | Steal succeeded, but copied only 2 L points, fewer than our 4, so no gain | Learned the US is weak in L research |
| Spy on China (L) | | Failed | The preview showed "Observe"; the log says failed |
| Effective basic research | 2.5 | **14.0** | **Medium research unlocked** (needs 8) |
| Next round | W 6 | W 10, 20 dice | 6 saved dice became +4 wealth |

**Rivals after Round 2**

| | Wealth | Talent | Own basic research | Effective basic research | Borders |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US | 9 | 32.4 | 8 | 15.5 | Open |
| China | 9 | 14.2 | 5 | 14.8 | Open |
| France | 5 | 2.3 | 3 | 14.3 | Closed |
| Russia | 6 (10 next round) | 2.0 | 2 | 14.0 | Open |

**What we learned:**
- Free-riding worked: rivals bought heavy basic research (US 7 successes, China 5, France 2), which unlocked medium research for us with only 2 points of our own.
- The US and China are also saving (wealth 9 each), so our budget lead is smaller than planned.
- US talent (32.4) gives it free basic research successes every round, its biggest advantage.
- Low-level steals on the US are useless while its L points are below ours.

**Why:** medium research opened for free, so we do not need to pour dice into basic research; at least two rounds remain, so saving still pays. Only one spy, because US L points are below ours, nobody has medium points yet, and France is closed.

**Reason to submit:** "Rivals' basic research unlocked advanced tech for us, so we free-ride on their public research, start medium research, and keep building our own know-how. We save the rest to grow our budget for the final rounds, and spy only on China because the US's low-level points are below ours."

**Test to check after Round 3:** our talent should be about 1.2 if opening did not help (then close in Round 4), or 2.0 or more if talent is flowing in (then stay open and consider education).
