## One page country assessment

*"Russia's national innovation system combines substantial technical capability with constraints on financing and institutional performance. In WIPO's fixed 2025 comparison, Russia ranks 60th overall, versus the United States at 3rd, China at 10th, and France at 13th. Russia's human-capital-and-research pillar ranks 28th, while institutions rank 131st. These indicators suggest that useful technical inputs coexist with weaknesses elsewhere in the innovation system; they do not establish that education alone determines national performance. [R5-R8; AI-1]"*

*"Education is therefore a resource to sustain and deploy. Factbook-derived entries report Russian literacy at 99.9% (2021), school life expectancy at 15 years (2023), and education expenditure at 4.2% of GDP (2023). Spending shares for the United States, China, and France are 5.4% (2021), 4.0% (2023), and 5.3% (2022). The mixed years and input measures cannot rank classroom quality or research productivity. Relative to the United States' university advantage and China's population scale in the game, Russia should seek the most productive affordable improvement; France's cheaper secondary-education upgrades make imitation of its spending mix unattractive. [R1-R4; G1; AI-1]"*

*"Technology strategy should connect existing knowledge to realizable output. The Factbook-derived Russian profile describes a large space program, illustrating concentrated technical capabilities. In the game this legacy is represented by +7 starting tacit knowledge, compared with France's +4; Russia also starts with the lowest wealth. Real-world GDP measures must remain separate: the same archive puts 2024 Russian GDP at $2.174 trillion at official exchange rates but $6.089 trillion in purchasing-power terms, above France's $3.732 trillion PPP figure. The instructor's normalized wealth, not a GDP calculation of our own, controls the game budget. ## Education recommendation for Tiger

*"I recommend selective education investment that improves the effectiveness of research without exhausting Russia's limited budget. The real-world evidence indicates a broad educational base, but literacy and expenditure are imperfect proxies for the quality of scientific training. The game's education mechanisms are more specific: secondary education raises domestic human capital, while tertiary education can also affect international talent when visitors are present. Because both improve research success, their value depends on the size and timing of the improvement relative to its cost and the number of research attempts remaining. Russia should compare the next affordable secondary and tertiary upgrade, rather than automatically buying either. [R1; G1; AI-2]"*

*"For Round 1, my proposed education envelope is 20% of newly issued spending points, provisionally divided equally between secondary and tertiary education. This is a planning allocation, not a claim about legal unit purchases. If the cheapest effective upgrade exceeds the envelope, choose the better affordable upgrade, or defer and retain the points; do not sacrifice every applied-research attempt to satisfy percentages. I would favor secondary education when it produces a larger domestic human-capital gain per point, and tertiary education when its research benefit or verified talent-flow effect is stronger. The United States' starting university advantage, China's population scaling, and France's inexpensive secondary upgrades show why a common education budget across countries would be inappropriate. [G1; AI-2]"*

*"This strategy is preferable to either neglecting education or maximizing it regardless of horizon. An early improvement can benefit several later research attempts, while a late investment may never repay its cost in a short game. Before increasing the education share, I will calculate the expected additional technology points generated over the remaining plausible rounds and compare that gain with direct research and retained wealth. If no productive payback is visible, I recommend maintaining current capacity and shifting the allocation toward research or savings. I will coordinate with Gloria before using university investment to justify openness, because visitor eligibility and talent retention must follow the actual game formula. [G1-G2; AI-2]"*

## Technology strategy recommendation for Steve

*"I recommend a focused applied-research strategy supported by only the basic research and human capital that make it productive. Russia's +7 starting tacit knowledge is a game advantage, not a guarantee of research success or automatic access to advanced categories. WIPO's indicators show a gap between technical capability and wider innovation performance. European Commission guidance updated in September 2026 also documents restrictions on external access to dual-use and advanced technology; the game does not explicitly implement these restrictions. We should use our legacy capabilities to generate affordable output, while avoiding an attempt to match the United States' budget or China's scale across all research paths. [R5, R9; G1; AI-3]"*

*"My Round 1 proposal assigns 45% of newly issued spending points to L applied research and 15% to basic research, leaving the rest for education and a reserve. L is the only initially available applied category in the supplied rules. Basic research has two distinct benefits: it helps meet advanced-path requirements and gives the investing country tacit knowledge, whereas foreign basic research contributes only the stated half-strength public spillover. I therefore recommend using foreign spillovers where available while retaining domestic basic research when its marginal unlock or tacit-knowledge value warrants the cost. If the next basic-research purchase cannot change a useful threshold or research probability, redirect it toward L research or saving. [G1; AI-3]"*

*"Once M or H becomes legally available, I will compare expected technology points and their final-score contribution per spending point, accounting for failure risk and the remaining horizon. Higher rewards do not establish a better expected return. We should move into M when it offers a better marginal payoff, and consider a bounded H position only when its prerequisites, odds, and potential contribution justify the risk. A short game favors earlier realizable returns; a longer game can reward investment in capabilities. Gilli and Gilli's argument about tacit and organizational knowledge cautions against treating foreign designs as a substitute for domestic capability. The game's espionage mechanism is more generous than that real-world process, so it should be evaluated on its own published terms. [C1; G1-G2; AI-3]"*

## Intelligence and immigration recommendation for Gloria

*"I recommend retaining closed immigration in Round 1 unless the actual talent-flow formula demonstrates a better alternative. Closure provides the stated defensive bonus, but I do not assume it prevents all talent outflow. Opening costs zero dice to change, yet affects human capital and espionage exposure. Russia's wealth and university quality may make openness less attractive than for the United States or France. The migration statistic cannot independently verify scientist flows. Separately, the UK government's July 2026 GRU profile describes Russian military intelligence cyber capabilities; this attributed assessment supports the relevance of intelligence, not a numerical espionage success rate. My recommendation rests on the game's actual flows and payoffs. [R1, R10; G1; AI-4]"*

If an affordable attempt has a better expected score contribution than a marginal research purchase or saving, the United States is a plausible initial candidate because its large budget may support valuable programs; this is a hypothesis, not knowledge of its secret allocations. France may be attractive because it begins open, and China remains relevant if its accumulated research becomes valuable. We may target at most two programs under the stated constraint, subject to the instructor's interpretation of targets. Successful observation reveals spending history; only a critical success copies the specified historical category earnings. Ordinary success must not be counted as stolen technology points. [G1; AI-4]"*

*"My operating rule is to purchase espionage only against a justified target and to reassess after any exposure or policy change. Critical failure reveals our behavior; its modeled penalty and any effect on other teams' future choices must enter the comparison. I will distinguish publicly visible basic research from hidden applied research, label uncertain estimates, and avoid asserting that another team is leading without evidence. Immigration policy will be reviewed with Tiger after education decisions, especially if stronger universities change the expected talent balance. The combined portfolio should protect and augment domestic capability; it should not displace research with a sequence of speculative intelligence gambles. [G1; AI-4]"*
