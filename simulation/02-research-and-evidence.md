### Real-world capabilities and constraints by portfolio

**Education:** R1's literacy, school-duration, and spending observations establish a broad educational baseline; R5 adds a research-system comparison. They do not establish the quality of every university, the distribution of skills, or the payback from any proposed reform. In the game, Russia's 0.60 talent flow means education purchases drain away, so the brief recommends no education spending despite this real-world strength.

**Technology:** Russia retains identifiable space capabilities (R1). Its broader system faces a concrete external technology constraint: the European Commission's export-restrictions guidance, updated September 25, 2026, covers dual-use and advanced technology restrictions concerning Russia. See **R9**, [European Commission guidance](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/export-related-restrictions-dual-use-goods-and-advanced-technologies_en). This supports a finding of constrained external technology access; it does not quantify lost innovation or imply that the simulation implements sanctions. A selective game strategy may differ from real-world policy because the game's objective, time horizon, and technology access are simplified.

**Intelligence:** The UK government's [GRU profile](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/profile-gru-cyber-and-hybrid-threat-operations/profile-gru-cyber-and-hybrid-threat-operations), **R10**, describes Russian military intelligence cyber and hybrid operations. This is an attributed UK government assessment, not a neutral measurement of all Russian intelligence activity or of successful commercial technology acquisition. It establishes a relevant state intelligence capability; it supplies no numerical justification for an espionage-heavy game allocation.

**Immigration:** The archive's aggregate migration balance is not a skilled-worker series. The best skilled-flow figure we found is the Russian digital development minister's December 2022 statement that about 100,000 IT specialists (roughly 10% of the sector) left in 2022 ([Interfax](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/86316/)); independent industry estimates were higher. We do not have verified current scientist-specific inflow/outflow numbers or a complete account of Russian immigration law. Avoid claiming that Russia prohibits immigration or that every emigrant is a scientist. The game compresses immigration and research security into one binary variable; real countries have multiple visa, research, and security policies. In the game, closure does not stop Russia's talent drain (flow 0.60 either way); it only blocks theft.

## How the rules changed on November 12

The two-page SIMULATION RULES left most mechanics unspecified. The rulebook resolves them, and several points in our first drafts no longer hold:

| Topic | Earlier rules or assumption | Rulebook (controls) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Score | TPs, unspent wealth, and "distance traveled" | (L + 2M + 3H TP) x TK multiplier + 2 sqrt(W). No distance-traveled term. |
| Saving | Unspent points carry over with interest | 2/3 of unspent dice adds to W; budget is 2W; last-round savings are lost |
| BR spillover | Foreign BR at 1/2 | Foreign BR at 0.75 |

## Course concepts that inform our recommendations

These are analytical connections, not additional game rules.

- **Tacit and organizational knowledge:** Gilli and Gilli (C1, pp. 141-143) argue that complex production makes imitation harder because knowledge is embedded in organizations and experience. This supports building domestic absorptive capability. It also exposes a simplification: in the game a successful steal copies a rival's points instantly.
- **National innovation systems:** Education, research, institutions, and application work through relationships. Our coordinated allocation is intended to address those relationships; it is not a claim that spending in each column yields a fixed multiplier.
- **Public goods and appropriability:** BR is public and 75% of it spills over to every rival, while TK stays private. This makes free-riding on rivals' BR attractive, which is why the brief delays Russia's own BR.
- **Diffusion versus invention:** Ding's *The diffusion deficit in scientific and technological power* (2024), DOI [10.1080/09692290.2023.2173633](https://doi.org/10.1080/09692290.2023.2173633), abstract and introduction, distinguishes creating innovation from widely adopting it. The published text is in `semester-readings/sources/ding-published.txt`. This is useful when reflecting on whether game TPs adequately measure national power; it does not add an adoption bonus to the game.

## Research limitations and checks

The national comparison supports the real-world sections of the brief; the game recommendations rest on the rulebook and our simulation, which simplifies rivals' behavior. Teacher announcements are not evidence of an actual team vote. The team should update research only if it changes a decision or corrects a material error; avoid adding uncited recent claims to make the brief appear more current.
