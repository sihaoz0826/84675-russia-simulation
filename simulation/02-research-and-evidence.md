### Real-world capabilities and constraints by portfolio

**Education:** R1's literacy, school-duration, and spending observations establish a broad educational baseline; R5 adds a research-system comparison. They do not establish the quality of every university, the distribution of skills, or the payback from any proposed reform. The draft proposes selective skills investment because the game attaches a research benefit to K, not because a national spending share proves which upgrade is best.

**Technology:** Russia retains identifiable space capabilities (R1). Its broader system faces a concrete external technology constraint: the European Commission's export-restrictions guidance, updated September 25, 2026, covers dual-use and advanced technology restrictions concerning Russia. See **R9**, [European Commission guidance](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/export-related-restrictions-dual-use-goods-and-advanced-technologies_en). This supports a finding of constrained external technology access; it does not quantify lost innovation or imply that the simulation implements sanctions. A selective game strategy may differ from real-world policy because the game's objective, time horizon, and technology access are simplified.

**Intelligence:** The UK government's [GRU profile](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/profile-gru-cyber-and-hybrid-threat-operations/profile-gru-cyber-and-hybrid-threat-operations), **R10**, describes Russian military intelligence cyber and hybrid operations. This is an attributed UK government assessment, not a neutral measurement of all Russian intelligence activity or of successful commercial technology acquisition. It establishes a relevant state intelligence capability; it supplies no numerical justification for an espionage-heavy game allocation.

**Immigration:** The archive's aggregate migration balance is not a skilled-worker series. We do not have verified current scientist-specific inflow/outflow numbers or a complete account of Russian immigration law. Avoid claiming that Russia prohibits immigration, that every emigrant is a scientist, or that game closure can prevent brain drain. The game compresses immigration and research security into one binary variable; real countries have multiple visa, research, and security policies. The brief's provisional closed posture follows the game starting conditions, rather than an asserted empirical immigration model.

Two textual inconsistencies must be resolved by the instructor: PREP p. 2 says 3-7 undeclared rounds while RULES p. 1 says 5-10; RULES says only L begins available but later says "M and L" can unlock, probably intending M and H. That probable typo is not authority to spend on H. RULES also says "six categories" while its numbered list runs 1-5; SE and TE are distinct entries in its explanatory table. Track both separately.

The espionage wording is especially important: it describes receiving historical TPs in a category "minus expenditures in the current round." TPs and expenditures may be different units. Do not silently reinterpret this as either all accumulated TPs or all prior-round TPs. Ask how the worksheet implements it.

## Course concepts that inform our recommendations

These are analytical connections, not additional game rules.

- **Tacit and organizational knowledge:** Gilli and Gilli (C1, pp. 141-143) argue that complex production makes imitation harder because knowledge is embedded in organizations and experience. This supports building domestic absorptive capability. It also exposes a simplification in automatic critical espionage rewards.
- **National innovation systems:** Education, research, institutions, and application work through relationships. Our coordinated allocation is intended to address those relationships; it is not a claim that spending in each column yields a fixed multiplier.
- **Public goods and appropriability:** BR is observable and partly spills over, while TK remains private in the game's rules. This creates an incentive to rely partly on others' BR, tempered by the value of own learning.
- **Diffusion versus invention:** Ding's *The diffusion deficit in scientific and technological power* (2024), DOI [10.1080/09692290.2023.2173633](https://doi.org/10.1080/09692290.2023.2173633), abstract and introduction, distinguishes creating innovation from widely adopting it. The published text is in `semester-readings/sources/ding-published.txt`. This is useful when reflecting on whether game TPs adequately measure national power; it does not add an adoption bonus to the game.
